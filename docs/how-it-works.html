Room3D · system notes · Oct 2026

How a phone video becomes a 3D room

You upload an .mp4. The server picks 120 sharp frames, works out where the camera was, estimates depth, and fuses everything into a point cloud. From that it finds the floor, walls, ceiling and furniture boxes. The browser draws the result with Three.js. It all runs on CPU and takes about a minute per video.

01The short version

Four steps, no jargon.

STEP 1Pick photosPull 120 frames from the video, keeping the least blurry ones.
STEP 2Find the cameraMatch features between photos to work out where the phone was for each one.
STEP 3Measure distanceAn AI model guesses how far away every pixel is. Combined with the camera path, this gives 3D dots.
STEP 4Understand the roomTall flat dot clusters become walls. An object detector labels furniture, which becomes boxes.

02System diagram

Where each piece runs, from your phone to the VPS and back.

Only the tunnel is public. FastAPI listens on localhost. Both services are systemd units with Restart=always, and the tunnel doesn't restart when the app does, so the URL stays the same.

03Upload protocol

Cloudflare rejects request bodies over 100 MB, so the file is sent in 8 MB pieces. If the connection drops, the browser asks the server how much it already has and carries on from there.

04The pipeline

This is what the worker runs for each video in pipeline.py. The percentages are the share of the progress bar each stage gets, which roughly matches where the time goes.

In local CPU tests a whole video took about 50–75 s. Depth runs once per frame and is the bottleneck.

1 · Frame picking

extract_frames()

The video is sampled at up to 6 fps, but never more than 120 frames total. For each frame it keeps, it decodes 3 neighbours and picks the least blurry, because motion blur breaks the camera tracking.

ffprobe duration → fps = min(6, 120/dur, source fps) · sharpness = variance of Laplacian · long side resized to 640 px

2 · Camera poses

run_sfm()

Structure-from-Motion finds the same corners in neighbouring frames and solves for where every camera was and which way it pointed. It also gives a sparse set of real 3D points. Frames it can't place are dropped.

pycolmap · SIFT 8192 feats, peak 0.004 · sequential matching, overlap 15 · incremental mapper, relaxed init (4°, 50 inliers) · single SIMPLE_RADIAL camera

3 · Depth + scale fix

estimate_depth()

A neural network estimates the distance to every pixel in metres. Its scale wobbles from frame to frame, so each depth map is rescaled until it agrees with the SfM points visible in that frame. One global factor then converts the whole scene to metres.

Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Indoor-Small (HF transformers, CPU torch) · per-frame median ratio vs SfM · depth *= g / s_frame

4 · Fuse + level the room

fuse() · gravity_align()

Every depth pixel is pushed out along its camera ray to become a coloured 3D point. Noisy edge pixels are thrown away and the cloud is thinned to one point per 3 cm cube. The cloud is then rotated so "up" is up and shifted so the floor sits at height 0.

flying-pixel filter on depth gradients · voxel 0.03 m · up = mean camera up-vector, refined with floor plane RANSAC (3 cm threshold)

5 · Walls + ceiling

detect_walls()

Looking down on the room in 6 cm cells, a wall shows up as a cell that has points at many different heights. Furniture is short, so it doesn't count. Lines are fitted through the tall cells, snapped to the room's two main directions, and split wherever there's a gap of more than 60 cm (usually a door). The ceiling is the highest dense layer of points.

tall cell = points in ≥55% of 15 cm height bins · up to 12 RANSAC lines, 8 cm inlier band · Manhattan snap within 12° · min segment 0.5 m · ceiling = top histogram peak above 1.8 m

6 · Furniture boxes

detect_objects() → fit_boxes()

YOLOE draws an outline around every object it recognises in each frame (it knows about 4,500 kinds of thing). The outline's pixels and their depths become 3D points. Detections of the same label that land close together are merged across frames, and a box is fitted, turned to line up with the walls. Beds, sofas, tables and similar items are extended down to the floor.

yoloe-11s-seg-pf (prompt-free) · conf ≥ 0.2 · names → ~25 canonical labels · merge if XZ distance < max(0.45 m, 0.6×extent) · needs ≥2 views · cv2.minAreaRect snapped to room yaw · 10–90th percentile extents

07Known limits

  • Fast or shaky video: COLMAP can't place blurry frames. If fewer than a handful get poses, the job fails with "only N/120 frames got camera poses". Walk slowly and avoid spinning in place.
  • Duplicate boxes: the same bed seen from opposite sides can merge into two boxes. A stricter merge step would fix this.
  • Walls you never filmed don't exist in the output. Neither do glass and mirrors, because depth models get them wrong.
  • Scale is approximate, since it depends on the depth model. Treat room sizes as close estimates, not tape-measure values.
  • The quick-tunnel URL changes if the tunnel service or the VPS restarts. A named Cloudflare tunnel would make it permanent. The site has no login.
